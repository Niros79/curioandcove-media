Airalo Alternatives: Saily vs Yesim vs GigSky (2026)

Published October 20, 2026 – 9-minute read

Short answer: Airalo is a fine default, but it isn't always the best fit. If you want security extras built in, look at Saily. If you'll use a lot of data or want to pay only for what you use, look at Yesim. For multi-country trips, compare regional plans from GigSky. And if you simply want unlimited data with no thinking, unlimited-per-day providers exist too. Below is how they differ, plus a quick Saily vs Yesim head to head.

Why look for an Airalo alternative?

Airalo is one of the best-known travel eSIM brands, with plans for a huge range of countries and an app that is easy the first time. People still shop around for a few practical reasons:

  • You can't choose the network. Airalo generally picks the local carrier for each country. If coverage matters where you're going (rural areas, islands, mountains), another provider may use a different network.
  • Unlimited plans have daily limits. Like most "unlimited" travel eSIMs, Airalo's unlimited plans give a daily amount of high-speed data and then slow down until the next day. Check the current fair-use limit before you buy.
  • Price varies by destination. No provider is cheapest everywhere. For the same country and data amount, prices can differ noticeably between brands.
  • Extras. Some travellers want things Airalo doesn't focus on, such as built-in ad blocking, pay-as-you-go data or a long-validity regional plan.

Saily

Saily comes from Nord Security, the team behind NordVPN. It covers a very large number of destinations with country, regional and global plans, and you keep one eSIM in the app and add new plans to it as you travel.

What makes it different are the security features built into the app: an ad blocker that filters many ads and trackers, web protection that blocks known malicious sites, and a virtual location option for browsing as if you were in another country. That won't replace a full VPN for everything, but it's a nice extra on public networks and on the road.

Good for: first-timers who want a clean app, people who already like Nord's products, and anyone who wants a bit of extra protection without installing more apps.

Keep in mind: Saily plans are data-only by default, so you call and message through apps like WhatsApp or FaceTime.

Yesim

Yesim covers 185+ destinations with country, regional and global plans. It stands out for two things: unlimited-data plans in many destinations and a pay-as-you-go option, where you top up a balance and spend it on data only as you use it.

Hotspot sharing is generally allowed, with the shared data counting against your plan, which makes it handy if you travel with a laptop or share data with family.

Good for: remote workers, longer stays, travellers who stream or video-call a lot, and people who hate guessing how many gigabytes they'll need.

Keep in mind: as with every unlimited plan, read the fair-use terms, and check speed and network details for your destination.

GigSky

GigSky has been doing travel data for longer than most brands on this list. Its regional and global plans are the reason to look at it: if you're crossing several borders in one trip, a single regional plan saves you from buying and installing a new eSIM in every country. GigSky is also often mentioned for data at sea on cruises, so check that option if it applies to you.

Good for: multi-country trips, rail trips across Europe or Asia, and cruise travellers.

Our rule of thumb: if you mostly need maps, messages and some social media, Saily keeps it simple and adds protection. If you'll be online all day, take Yesim's unlimited or pay-as-you-go route. Either way, compare the price for your exact country and trip length, because it changes by destination.

How to choose in 3 minutes

  1. Check your phone. It must support eSIM and be carrier-unlocked. If it doesn't, use a physical travel SIM such as Drimsim.
  2. Estimate your data. Maps, chat and email: about 3–5 GB a week. Add social media and photo uploads: 5–10 GB. Video calls or streaming: 10 GB+ or unlimited. (More in our eSIM guide.)
  3. Count the countries. One country: a local plan is usually cheapest. Several: compare one regional plan against separate local plans.
  4. Compare two or three providers for your exact destination, same data and same number of days.
  5. Read the small print: validity, when the days start counting, hotspot rules and fair-use limits on unlimited plans.

Setup tips that apply to any provider

  • Install the eSIM at home on Wi-Fi, a day or two before you fly, and label it "Travel".
  • Switch it on for data when you land, and turn off data roaming on your home SIM to avoid surprise charges.
  • Don't delete the eSIM until you're home: you may want to top it up.
  • Download offline maps before you leave, just in case.
  • On airport, hotel or café Wi-Fi, use a VPN for anything sensitive. See do you need a VPN when you travel?

Frequently asked questions

What is the best alternative to Airalo?

It depends on the trip. Saily is a strong pick if you want a simple app with security extras, Yesim if you need lots of data or pay-as-you-go, and GigSky for multi-country trips. Compare the price for your exact destination.

Is Saily better than Airalo?

Not better for everyone, but different: Saily adds an ad blocker, web protection and virtual location inside the app. Airalo has a very wide choice of plans. Compare both for your destination.

Saily or Yesim: which is better?

Saily suits short and medium trips and first-timers who like built-in security features. Yesim suits heavy data users and longer stays thanks to unlimited and pay-as-you-go options.

Are unlimited eSIM plans really unlimited?

Usually not completely. Most unlimited travel eSIMs give a daily amount of high-speed data and slow down afterwards, and some cap hotspot use. Check the fair-use terms before you buy.

Do travel eSIMs include a phone number?

Most are data-only. You can still call and message through WhatsApp, FaceTime or Signal, and keep your home SIM for calls and texts.
