Best eSIM for Travel: Airalo vs Saily vs Yesim vs GigSky

A travel eSIM gives you mobile data the moment you land, without hunting for a SIM shop or paying your carrier's roaming fees. You buy it online, scan a QR code, and you're connected. Here's how eSIMs work, when they're worth it, and how the best-known providers compare.

What is a travel eSIM?

An eSIM is a SIM card built into your phone. Instead of swapping a plastic card, you download a data plan from an app or a QR code. Your regular SIM stays in place, so you can keep your number for calls and texts while the eSIM handles mobile data abroad.

Most travel eSIMs are data-only. You get internet for maps, messaging apps, ride-hailing and calls over WhatsApp or FaceTime, but not a local phone number.

Before you buy: check your phone

  • eSIM support: most recent iPhones, Google Pixels and Samsung Galaxy flagships support eSIM. Check your phone's settings for "Add eSIM" or look up your model.
  • Unlocked: your phone must not be locked to your carrier. If you bought it on a contract, ask your carrier.
  • Install before you leave: download the eSIM at home on Wi-Fi, and switch it on when you land.

How to choose the right eSIM plan

  • Country, regional or global: a single-country plan is usually cheapest. If you're visiting several countries, a regional plan (Europe, Asia) saves you buying several eSIMs.
  • Data amount: maps and messaging use little data; video calls, streaming and hotspot use a lot. For a week of normal use, a few gigabytes is often enough.
  • Validity: make sure the plan lasts as long as your trip, and check when the clock starts (at installation or on first connection).
  • Top-ups: it's easier to add data to the same eSIM than to install a new one.
  • Hotspot: if you want to share data with a laptop or a travel partner, check that tethering is allowed.

Airalo vs Saily vs Yesim vs GigSky

All four sell data plans for most countries in the world through an app. Prices change often and vary by destination, so always compare the plan for your exact country and trip length.

Airalo

Airalo is one of the best-known travel eSIM brands, with plans for a very wide range of countries plus regional and global packages. It's a good default choice if you want lots of options and an app that's easy for first-timers.

Saily

Saily comes from Nord Security, the company behind NordVPN. It focuses on a simple app with country, regional and global plans, and adds online security features inside the app. A good fit if you like the idea of one trusted brand handling both data and security.

GigSky

Which one should you pick?

  • First eSIM, short trip: Airalo, for its wide choice and simple setup.
  • You care about online security: Saily.
  • You need a lot of data: look at Yesim's unlimited plans.
  • Several countries in one trip: compare regional and global plans from GigSky and Airalo.
  • Your phone doesn't support eSIM: a physical travel SIM such as Drimsim works in many countries.

Setting up your eSIM in 5 steps

  1. Buy the plan in the provider's app or website.
  2. At home, on Wi-Fi, install the eSIM from the app or by scanning the QR code.
  3. Label it ("Travel") so you can tell it apart from your main line.
  4. On arrival, switch the travel eSIM on for mobile data and turn data roaming on for that line only.
  5. Keep your main SIM on for calls and texts if you need them, but switch its data off to avoid roaming charges.

These are rough guides: video is what eats data fastest. If you're unsure, pick a slightly bigger plan or one you can top up from the app.

Tips to make your data last

  • Download offline maps and playlists on hotel Wi-Fi before you head out.
  • Turn off automatic app updates and photo backup on mobile data.
  • Use your travel eSIM for data and keep your home SIM on for calls and texts only if you need it, with data roaming switched off.
  • On public Wi-Fi, use a VPN; see NordVPN vs Surfshark for travel.

Frequently asked questions

Does my phone support eSIM?

Most recent iPhones, Google Pixels and Samsung Galaxy phones do, but some regional models and older phones don't. Your phone must also be carrier-unlocked. On iPhone, look for "Add eSIM" under Settings › Mobile/Cellular; on Android, search the settings for "eSIM".

Will I keep my WhatsApp and my phone number?

Yes. WhatsApp is linked to your number, not your SIM, so it keeps working over the eSIM's data. Your home SIM can stay in the phone for calls and texts; just switch off its data roaming.

When should I install my travel eSIM?

Install it at home on Wi-Fi a few days before you fly, then switch it on for data when you land. Many plans only start counting days once they connect at your destination.

Does a travel eSIM come with a phone number?

Most travel eSIMs are data-only. You can still call and message through apps like WhatsApp, FaceTime or Signal over data.

Can I use a travel eSIM as a hotspot?

Usually yes, but check the plan details: a few providers limit tethering on some plans.

Which travel eSIM is best?

It depends on the trip: Saily for simple plans with security features, Yesim for lots of data, and GigSky for multi-country trips. See the comparison above.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Deleting the eSIM after the trip if you might top it up later. You often can't reinstall it.
  • Leaving data roaming on for your home SIM, which can lead to a surprise bill.
  • Buying too little data for a long trip. Top-ups usually cost more per gigabyte than a bigger plan.
  • Waiting until you land to install it, when you may not have Wi-Fi.
