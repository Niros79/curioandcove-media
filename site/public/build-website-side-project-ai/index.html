How to Build a Website for Your Side Project with AI (No Coding Needed)

Published October 1, 2026 – 8-minute read

A website used to mean hiring a developer. Today, an AI website builder can turn a few sentences about your project into a working site in an afternoon. Here's how to do it properly, wherever you live, without wasting money.

Why your side project needs its own website

Social media accounts are rented space: the algorithm decides who sees your posts, and an account can be restricted overnight. A website is yours. It makes you look credible to customers, it can be found on Google, and it gives you one link to put everywhere, from your Instagram bio to your email signature.

What you actually need

  • A domain name: your address, like yourname.com. A .com usually costs around $10 to $20 a year. Country endings like .co.uk or .ca work well if your customers are local.
  • Hosting: the service that keeps your site online. Beginner plans often start at a few dollars a month.
  • A website builder: the tool you design the site with. Many hosting companies now include one with AI built in.

Most beginners are best served by a provider that bundles all three, so there's one account, one bill and one place to ask for help.

Step 1: Decide what the site must do

Before touching any tool, write one sentence: "This site helps [who] to [do what]." Then decide the single action you want visitors to take, such as booking a call, buying a product, or joining your email list. Every page should point toward that action.

Step 2: Choose a name and domain

  • Keep it short, easy to spell and easy to say out loud.
  • Avoid hyphens and numbers.
  • Check that the same name is free on the social networks you use.

Step 3: Pick a builder with AI

When comparing providers, look past the headline price. Check:

  • The renewal price. Introductory deals are often much cheaper than the price you'll pay from the second year.
  • What's included: a free SSL certificate (the padlock in the browser), a professional email address, and whether the domain is free for the first year.
  • The AI tools: can it generate a full first draft of your site, write and rewrite text, and create images?
  • Support: 24/7 chat matters more than you think when something breaks on launch day.

Hostinger is one of the popular budget-friendly options for beginners. It combines hosting, domains and an AI website builder in one place, and serves customers in most countries. As with any provider, compare its renewal price before committing.

Step 4: Let AI build the first draft, then make it yours

Describe your project to the AI builder in a few sentences: what you offer, who it's for, and the style you like. It will generate a layout, sections and placeholder text in minutes.

Then do the part AI can't do for you:

  • Rewrite the text in your own words. Visitors can tell when copy sounds generic.
  • Replace stock photos with real ones of you, your products or your work wherever you can.
  • Check every claim. AI sometimes invents details, like services you don't offer.

Step 5: The five pages every small site needs

  1. Home: what you do, for whom, and the main action.
  2. About: who's behind it and why you can be trusted.
  3. Offer or services: what people can buy or book, with clear prices if possible.
  4. Contact: an email address or form.
  5. Privacy policy: required in many countries if you collect any personal data, even just emails.

Step 6: Launch and get found

  • Add your site to Google Search Console so Google can find and index it.
  • Put your web address in every social media bio.
  • Add an email signup form, so visitors who aren't ready to buy can still stay in touch.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Spending weeks on design before you have a single visitor. Launch simple, improve later.
  • Choosing the cheapest plan without checking the renewal price.
  • Publishing AI text without reading it.
  • Forgetting mobile: most visitors will see your site on a phone, so preview it there.

Tools that help after launch

  • Collect emails from day one. A newsletter is the one audience no algorithm can take away. Kit is free for up to 10,000 subscribers; our newsletter guide shows how to set it up in an afternoon.
  • Protect your logins. Your domain, builder and email accounts are the keys to the whole site. A password manager such as NordPass gives each one a unique password and stores your two-factor backup codes safely.
  • Use AI for the boring bits. See 12 tasks you can hand to a free AI assistant.

Quick recap

  • You need a domain, hosting and a builder, ideally from one provider.
  • Let AI create the first draft, then rewrite it in your voice.
  • Start with five pages, launch quickly and add an email signup.
