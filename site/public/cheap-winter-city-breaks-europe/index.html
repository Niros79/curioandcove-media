Cheap Winter City Breaks in Europe: 10 Cities Worth the Trip

Published October 23, 2026 – 8 min read

Winter is the quiet secret of European travel. Outside the Christmas and New Year weeks, flights and hotels drop to some of their lowest prices of the year, the queues disappear, and cities feel like they belong to the people who live there. Here are ten cities that are especially good value between November and March, and how to keep the whole trip cheap.

These picks work whether you are hopping over from the UK, flying in from North America, or adding a few cities to a longer trip from Australia or New Zealand.

The short version

  • Want mild weather? Go south: Lisbon, Porto, Seville, Valencia or Athens.
  • Want Christmas markets and snow? Go east: Prague, Budapest, Kraków, Tallinn or Ljubljana.
  • Cheapest weeks: mid-January to early March, and November. Avoid the two weeks around Christmas and New Year.
  • Biggest saver: book a flight and hotel together, and stay flexible by a day or two.

Mild winter city breaks

If you want to walk around without a heavy coat, southern Europe is the place. Expect cool evenings and some rain, but plenty of days that feel like spring somewhere further north.

Lisbon, Portugal

One of the warmest capitals in Europe in winter, with plenty of bright days. Ride the old trams, walk the Alfama hills, and take the train to Sintra or the coast at Cascais. Winter is low season, so hotels are noticeably cheaper than in summer. Day trips and tours: things to do in Lisbon on Viator.

Porto, Portugal

Smaller and usually cheaper than Lisbon. Cross the river to the port cellars in Gaia, eat a francesinha, and wander the tiled streets. Bring a rain jacket: winter here is green for a reason. Day trips and tours: things to do in Porto on Viator.

Seville, Spain

Often the sunniest pick on this list, with orange trees in the streets and short queues at the Alcázar and the cathedral. Evenings are cool, days are often pleasant enough for a terrace lunch. Day trips and tours: things to do in Seville on Viator.

Valencia, Spain

Beach walks in January, the futuristic City of Arts and Sciences, and paella where it was born. Good low-cost flight links from much of Europe.

Athens, Greece

The Acropolis without the summer heat or crowds. Winter is a great time for the museums and long walks between the ancient sites, and prices drop well below peak season. Day trips and tours: things to do in Athens on Viator.

Christmas-market and snowy city breaks

Central and Eastern Europe are cold in winter, but that is part of the charm, and these cities are often much cheaper than Paris, London or Amsterdam for food, drinks and hotels.

Prague, Czech Republic

Fairy-tale in the frost and one of the classic Christmas-market cities. Food and drink are still good value compared with Western Europe. Go early in the morning to have the Charles Bridge almost to yourself. Day trips and tours: things to do in Prague on Viator.

Budapest, Hungary

The thermal baths are made for winter: sitting in hot outdoor pools while it is freezing around you is the whole point. Add the Christmas market by the basilica and the ruin bars. Day trips and tours: things to do in Budapest on Viator.

Kraków, Poland

Among the best-value city breaks in Europe. A huge main square with a Christmas market, a lively old Jewish quarter, and day trips to the Wieliczka salt mine. Day trips and tours: things to do in Kraków on Viator.

Tallinn, Estonia

A medieval old town that looks like a snow globe in December, and one of the oldest Christmas-tree traditions in Europe. Days are short, so plan cosy cafés and museums.

Ljubljana, Slovenia

Small, walkable and festive, with the river lit up in December. Easy to combine with a day at Lake Bled.

How to keep a winter city break cheap

1. Look at flight and hotel together

Packages are often cheaper than booking the two separately, especially for short breaks. Compare a few cities at once with Trip.com flight + hotel deals, then check the same dates for the flight alone so you know what you are saving.

Compare winter flight + hotel deals

2. Search flexible dates and nearby airports

Moving your trip by one or two days, or flying into a second airport, often changes the price more than anything else. Kiwi.com is good for this because it combines airlines that do not normally sell together. Our guide on how to find cheap flights covers the full method, and the cheapest time to fly to Europe shows how prices move month by month.

3. Travel with hand luggage only

Low-cost airlines make much of their money on bags. A winter break of three or four days fits in a cabin bag if you pack layers instead of bulky jumpers.

4. Choose a central hotel, skip the taxis

A slightly cheaper hotel far from the centre often costs more once you add transport. In winter, being able to walk back to your room for a break is worth a lot. Search hotels on Trip.com and filter by distance to the old town.

5. Decide on a city pass before you go, not after

Passes can save money in museum-heavy cities, but only if you would really visit most of what they include. Our guide are city passes worth it? shows how to do the quick maths. For single tickets and skip-the-line entries, compare prices on Viator.

6. Get mobile data before you land

Short days mean you will lean on maps and transit apps after dark. An eSIM is usually far cheaper than roaming. See our comparison of the best eSIMs for travel.

7. Sort out travel insurance when you book

A slip on icy cobblestones or a cancelled flight can cost far more than the trip itself. Buy cover soon after booking and compare a couple of quotes; SafetyWing is one option, sold as a flexible subscription you can stop when you get home.

What to pack for a winter city break

  • Waterproof shoes with a good grip: cobblestones get slippery.
  • Layers: a thermal top, a warm mid layer and a windproof jacket beat one heavy coat.
  • Hat, gloves and a scarf for the eastern cities.
  • A small umbrella or rain jacket for Portugal and Spain.
  • A power bank, because cold weather drains phone batteries faster.

Frequently asked questions

Which European city is warmest in winter?

Of the big city-break destinations, Seville, Lisbon, Valencia and Athens are usually the mildest, with many pleasant days even in January.

When are flights to Europe cheapest in winter?

Usually from mid-January to early March, and in November. The weeks around Christmas and New Year are among the most expensive of the year.

Is it worth visiting Europe in winter?

Yes, especially for cities. Prices are lower, museums and landmarks are quieter, and December brings the Christmas markets. Just plan for shorter days and pack for rain or cold.

How many days do I need for a winter city break?

Three or four days is enough for most of the cities on this list. Smaller places like Ljubljana or Tallinn work well in two or three days.
