The Cheapest Time to Fly to Europe (Month by Month)

Published October 6, 2026 – 9-minute read

This article contains affiliate links. If you buy through them, we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. How this works.

The same seat to Paris, Rome or Lisbon can cost hundreds less depending on the month you fly. The good news: the pattern is predictable. Here's when flights to Europe are cheapest, how far ahead to book, and which tricks actually make a difference.

The advice below works whether you're flying from North America, Australia and New Zealand, or hopping over from the UK and Ireland. Where the timing differs, we say so.

The short answer

  • Cheapest months: January, February and November, outside the Christmas and New Year weeks. Early March is often good value too.
  • Best value overall: the shoulder seasons, April to May and September to October. Fares are moderate, the weather is pleasant and the crowds are smaller.
  • Most expensive: late June to August, Easter and the two weeks around Christmas.
  • When to book: start watching fares about four to six months out and book when you see a good price. For summer and holidays, book earlier.
  • Biggest single saver: being flexible by a few days, or flying into a different airport.

Flying from Australia or New Zealand?

Your cheapest windows are slightly different because your own school holidays matter as much as Europe's. February to March and October to November are usually the lowest-priced months, while June, July and December tend to be the most expensive.

Flying from the UK or Ireland?

Short flights to the continent follow the same seasons, but the school holidays move prices the most. Fly just before or after half-term and the summer break and you'll often pay a fraction of the peak fare, especially with low-cost airlines.

How far in advance to book

You'll find very different answers online, because the studies use different routes and years. Some booking-site data shows good international deals only a few weeks out, while long-haul fares to Europe are often cheapest a few months ahead. What they agree on: the very last two weeks are rarely cheap, and booking a year ahead rarely gets you the lowest fare either.

A simple rule that works for most trips:

  • Off-season and shoulder season: start watching about four months before, and book when the fare drops into a range you're happy with.
  • Summer: book around five to seven months ahead.
  • Christmas, Easter and school holidays: book as early as your plans allow. The cheap seats on popular dates go first.

Fares are sold in batches: when the cheapest seats on a flight sell out, the next batch costs more. That's why a good price is worth taking instead of waiting for a perfect one.

The cheapest days to fly

The day you fly matters more than the day you book. The old "book on a Tuesday" tip makes little difference today. Midweek departures, usually Tuesday and Wednesday, are often cheaper on long-haul routes, while some recent booking data has found Fridays cheapest for certain markets. The practical lesson: always look at a price calendar for the days around your ideal date instead of searching a single day.

7 tricks that really lower the fare

1. Search a whole month, not one date

Use the flexible dates or price calendar view on a flight search site. Moving your trip by one or two days is the easiest saving there is.

2. Try different arrival airports

Big hubs such as London, Paris, Amsterdam, Frankfurt, Madrid, Lisbon and Dublin usually have the most transatlantic and long-haul competition. Flying into a hub and taking a train or a short low-cost flight onward is often cheaper than flying straight to a smaller city.

3. Mix airlines

Combining a long-haul flight with a separate low-cost flight inside Europe can cut the price a lot. Kiwi.com specialises in these combinations and puts together airlines that don't normally sell connections together. If you book separate tickets, leave plenty of time between flights and check the baggage rules on both.

4. Open-jaw tickets

Flying into one city and home from another, for example into Rome and out of Paris, often costs about the same as a regular return and saves you a day of backtracking.

5. Count the real price

A cheap fare without a checked bag or seat selection can end up costing more than a slightly higher fare that includes them. Compare the total, not the headline number.

6. Set a price alert

Once you know your route, set an alert and let the fares come to you. When you get a price that's clearly below what you've seen before, book.

7. Compare more than one site

Different search engines find different combinations. Trip.com compares airlines worldwide and often has good fares for routes via Asia and the Middle East, while Kiwi.com is strong on creative routings and budget airlines.

If your flight is delayed or cancelled

Flights from the EU, and flights into the EU on a European airline, come with strong passenger rights. If you arrive three or more hours late, or your flight is cancelled at short notice, you may be owed up to €600 per person, whatever your nationality.
