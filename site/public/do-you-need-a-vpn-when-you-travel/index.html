Do You Need a VPN When You Travel? An Honest Guide

Published October 13, 2026 · Updated October 17, 2026 – 9-minute read

You've probably heard that you "must" use a VPN abroad. The truth is more useful: a VPN solves a few specific travel problems very well, and does nothing for others. Here's when it's worth it, when it isn't, and how to set one up before you leave.

What a VPN actually does

A VPN (virtual private network) encrypts the connection between your device and a VPN server, and makes your traffic appear to come from that server's location. That has two practical effects for travellers:

  • Privacy on shared networks: people on the same hotel, café or airport Wi-Fi can't easily see or tamper with what you send.
  • A "home" location: you can connect through a server in your own country, which helps when an app or website behaves differently abroad.

What it doesn't do: it won't make you anonymous, protect you from phishing emails, or stop you from installing a bad app. Good habits still matter.

When a VPN is genuinely useful on a trip

  • You rely on public Wi-Fi. Hotels, airports and cafés often run open networks. A VPN adds a layer of protection when you check email, book things or log in to accounts.
  • Your banking app blocks foreign logins. Some banks and services flag or limit logins from abroad. Connecting through a server at home can avoid security lockouts (keep your bank's own travel notice settings up to date too).
  • You want your usual services to work normally. Some websites show different prices, content or language depending on where you are. Note that streaming services set their own rules on location, so check their terms.
  • You work remotely. If you handle work files or client data, many employers expect or require a VPN on public networks.

When you probably don't need one

  • You use mobile data from a travel eSIM and avoid public Wi-Fi. Mobile networks are harder to snoop on than an open café network.
  • You're only browsing well-known sites over HTTPS and never log in on shared Wi-Fi.
  • It's a short trip and you don't need any home-only apps.

Even then, many travellers keep a VPN installed "just in case", because it costs little and the hotel Wi-Fi is often the only option.

A note on local laws

Using a VPN is legal in most countries, but a few restrict or ban VPN use, and rules can change. Always check the current rules for your destination before you rely on one.

Don't forget your passwords

A VPN protects the connection, not your accounts. The single best security habit before a trip is using unique passwords, so one leaked login can't unlock everything else. A password manager such as NordPass stores them for you, fills them in on any device and lets you share logins safely with a travel partner. Set it up before you leave: 5 steps

  1. Choose a provider and install the app on every device you're taking.
  2. Log in at home and test it once, so you're not troubleshooting on airport Wi-Fi.
  3. Turn on the setting that connects automatically on untrusted Wi-Fi networks.
  4. Save a favourite server in your home country for banking and home apps.
  5. Turn on two-factor authentication for email and banking, and check you can still receive the codes abroad.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a VPN when I travel?

It's most useful when you rely on hotel, airport or café Wi-Fi, use banking apps that block foreign logins, or work remotely. If you mostly use mobile data from a travel eSIM, you may not need one.

Is it safe to use hotel Wi-Fi without a VPN?

For browsing well-known sites over HTTPS it's usually fine, but shared networks are open to strangers. A VPN encrypts everything you send, which is safer for email, banking and work.

Will a VPN let me use my banking app abroad?

Often, yes. Connecting to a server in your home country can stop your bank from flagging a foreign login. Keep your bank's travel notice up to date too.

Which VPN is best for travel?

NordVPN suits solo travellers who want the most established brand; Surfshark suits families because it covers unlimited devices. See NordVPN vs Surfshark.

Are VPNs legal everywhere?

They're legal in most countries, but a few restrict or ban them. Check your destination's current rules before you travel.

Common mistakes

  • Installing the VPN after you arrive, when the app store or website may be harder to reach.
  • Leaving it off on hotel Wi-Fi because "it's just the hotel".
  • Thinking a VPN replaces good passwords. It doesn't.
