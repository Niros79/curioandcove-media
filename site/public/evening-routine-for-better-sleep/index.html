A Simple Evening Routine for Better Sleep (That You'll Actually Keep)

Published October 23, 2026 – 6 min read

Most sleep advice fails because it asks you to change everything at once. A good evening routine is not a perfect checklist. It is a short, repeatable wind-down that tells your body the day is over. Here is a simple 60-minute routine, why each step helps, and how to make it stick.

The routine at a glance

WhenWhat to do
Same time every morningGet up at a consistent time, even at weekends (within about an hour).
AfternoonLast coffee or strong tea by early afternoon.
2 to 3 hours before bedFinish your main meal; go easy on alcohol.
60 minutes before bedDim the lights, put the phone on charge outside the bedroom.
45 minutes beforeTidy tomorrow: a short list and anything you need laid out.
30 minutes beforeWarm shower or bath, then something calm: reading, stretching, journaling.
BedtimeCool, dark, quiet room. If you can't sleep after a while, get up briefly and try again.

Step by step

1. Start with your wake-up time

It sounds backwards, but a fixed wake-up time is the anchor for the whole routine. When you get up at the same time every day, your body starts to feel sleepy at a consistent time in the evening. Get some daylight in the first half hour after waking if you can.

2. Dim the lights an hour before bed

Bright overhead light in the evening tells your brain it is still daytime. Switch to lamps or warm, low lighting for the last hour. It is one of the easiest changes and one of the most noticeable.

3. Give your phone a bedtime

The problem is less the screen itself than what is on it: messages, news and endless scrolling keep your mind switched on. Charge the phone outside the bedroom and use a cheap alarm clock instead. If that feels like too much, start with the phone face-down across the room.

4. Empty your head onto paper

If your mind races in bed, spend five minutes writing tomorrow's to-do list and anything that is bothering you. Getting it out of your head and onto paper makes it easier to let go. Our guide on how to start journaling has simple prompts, and a planner such as The Happy Planner makes the habit pleasant to keep.

5. Warm up, then cool down

A warm shower or bath before bed helps many people feel sleepy, partly because the body cools down afterwards. Keep the bedroom itself cool and well aired.

6. Make the bedroom for sleeping

  • Dark: blackout curtains or a comfortable eye mask.
  • Quiet: earplugs or a fan or white-noise machine if you live somewhere noisy.
  • Cool: most people sleep better in a cooler room. If you or your partner sleep hot, a temperature-controlled mattress cover such as Eight Sleep is a premium option that cools or warms each side of the bed separately.
  • Comfortable: a pillow that suits your sleeping position is worth more than most gadgets.

7. If you can't fall asleep

Lying in bed frustrated teaches your brain that bed is a place for worrying. If you are still awake after what feels like a long while, get up, go somewhere dim and do something quiet, then go back when you feel sleepy.

How to make the routine stick

  • Start with one step. Pick the change that feels easiest and do only that for a week.
  • Link it to something you already do: "after I brush my teeth, I write tomorrow's list".
  • Set a wind-down alarm 60 minutes before bed, not just a wake-up alarm.
  • Be kind about bad nights. One late night does not undo the habit. Just get up at your usual time.

This article is general information, not medical advice. If you regularly struggle to sleep, snore loudly, or feel exhausted despite enough time in bed, talk to a doctor.

Frequently asked questions

How long before bed should I start winding down?

Around 60 minutes works for most people. Even 20 to 30 minutes of dim light and no phone is a good start.

Is it better to fix bedtime or wake-up time?

Wake-up time. A consistent wake-up time anchors your body clock, and a natural bedtime tends to follow.

What should I do if I can't fall asleep?

If you have been lying awake for a long while, get up, sit somewhere dim and do something calm, then return to bed when you feel sleepy.
