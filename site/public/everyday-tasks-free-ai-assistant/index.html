12 Everyday Tasks You Can Hand to a Free AI Assistant

Published September 30, 2026 · Updated October 16, 2026 – 7-minute read

You don't need to be "techy" to get real use out of an AI assistant. Free versions of tools like ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini can take a surprising amount of small, tedious work off your plate. Here are twelve everyday tasks to try, with an example of what to type for each.

Before you start: three ground rules

  • Check anything important. AI assistants can sound confident and still be wrong, especially about facts, numbers, laws and health. Treat answers as a first draft.
  • Keep private details out. Don't paste passwords, full account numbers, ID numbers or other people's personal information. A password manager such as NordPass keeps your logins safe, so you never need to type them into a chat or a note.
  • Be specific. The more context you give (who, what, how long, what tone), the better the result.

1. Plan a week of meals

Try: "Plan five simple weeknight dinners for a family of four, each under 30 minutes, with no mushrooms. Then give me one combined grocery list grouped by store section."

2. Use up what's in the fridge

Try: "I have chicken thighs, half a bag of spinach, rice, a lemon and feta. What can I make tonight?"

3. Rewrite an awkward email or message

Paste your draft and ask: "Make this more polite but still clear. I need a reply by Friday." It's especially helpful for complaints, reminders and saying no.

4. Summarise something long

Paste an article, a school newsletter or terms and conditions and ask for the five main points in plain language. For anything that affects money or legal rights, still read the original.

5. Explain a confusing letter or bill

Try: "Explain what this letter is asking me to do, in simple terms." Remove your personal details first, and confirm anything important with the organisation that sent it.

6. Build a simple budget

List your monthly income and regular expenses (no account numbers) and ask it to organise them into categories and point out where most of the money goes. It's a good starting point for a budget you then adjust yourself.

7. Make a study plan for a new skill

Try: "I want to learn basic Excel in four weeks with 20 minutes a day. Make me a day-by-day plan with free resources." This works for languages, photography, coding and almost any hobby.

8. Practise a conversation

Ask it to play the other person: a job interviewer, a landlord, or someone you need to have a difficult talk with. Practising out loud, even with an AI, makes the real thing easier.

9. Brainstorm gifts or party ideas

Try: "Gift ideas under $30 for a 70-year-old who loves gardening and crossword puzzles." Use the answers as inspiration and check prices yourself.

10. Organise a messy to-do list

Paste everything that's in your head and ask it to sort it by urgency and group similar tasks. Seeing the list organised often makes it feel smaller.

11. Plan a day trip or weekend

Give it the place, your budget and what you enjoy, and ask for a relaxed itinerary. Always double-check opening hours, prices and travel times on official websites, because they change. For a bigger trip, our trip planning checklist shows what to book and when.

12. Translate and understand

AI assistants are good at translating everyday messages and explaining phrases in context. For official documents, use a professional translator.

What AI assistants are not good for

They're not doctors, lawyers or financial advisors, and they can make mistakes or invent details. Use them to save time on drafts, ideas and organisation, and keep human judgement for the decisions that matter.

Quick recap

  • Great for drafts, plans, summaries, lists and practice.
  • Give plenty of context and ask follow-up questions.
  • Keep private data out and double-check anything important.
