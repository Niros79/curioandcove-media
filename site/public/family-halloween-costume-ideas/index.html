Halloween Costume Ideas for the Whole Family (Easy Group Themes)

Published October 13, 2026 – 7-minute read

Halloween falls on a Saturday this year, which means more parties, more trick-or-treating and more chances for a family costume. Here are easy family and group themes, quick no-sew ideas from things you already own, and a few tips so the costumes actually survive the night.

How to pick a family theme in 10 minutes

  1. Start with the youngest. Small children change their minds less if the theme is built around what they love: animals, space, food, vehicles.
  2. Pick a theme with roles, so everyone gets a different character rather than the same outfit.
  3. Check the weather. If it's cold where you live, choose costumes that work over a coat or warm layers.
  4. Decide buy, build or mix. Most families buy one or two key pieces (a hat, a wig, a mask) and make the rest.

15 family and group costume themes

Easy and cute

  • Farm animals: a cow, a pig, a sheep and a farmer. Ears, a nose and matching colours do most of the work.
  • Breakfast table: bacon, eggs, toast and a cup of coffee.
  • Garden: flowers, a bee, a ladybug and a gardener with a watering can.
  • Crayon box: each person wears one bold colour with a paper label. Great for big groups.
  • Weather forecast: a sun, a rain cloud, a rainbow and a weather presenter.

Adventure

  • Space crew: astronauts, an alien and a planet.
  • Pirates: a captain, a parrot, a treasure chest and the crew.
  • Safari: an explorer with binoculars, a lion, a zebra and a giraffe.
  • Under the sea: a diver, an octopus, a jellyfish (an umbrella with ribbons) and a fish.
  • Knights and dragons: a castle guard, a knight and a friendly dragon.

Classic spooky

  • Haunted house family: a vampire, a mummy, a witch and a black cat.
  • Skeleton crew: matching skeleton outfits, easy to find in every size.
  • Witches' coven: hats, capes and a cauldron for the treats.
  • Mad scientists: lab coats, goggles and wild hair.
  • Pumpkin patch: orange tops and green hats, with one scarecrow parent.

Last-minute ideas from your wardrobe

  • Ghost: an old white sheet with eye holes, or a white T-shirt and face paint for young children who don't like covered faces.
  • Mummy: white leggings and top wrapped loosely with strips of an old sheet or crepe bandage.
  • Black cat: black clothes, paper ears on a headband and a painted nose.
  • Tourist: a loud shirt, sunglasses, a camera and a map. Perfect for the parent who forgot.
  • Scarecrow: a flannel shirt, jeans, a straw hat and a few bits of raffia.

Safety tips for trick-or-treating

  • Be seen: add reflective tape, a glow stick or a small torch, especially to dark costumes.
  • Face paint over masks for small children, so they can see properly. Test the paint on a small patch of skin a day before.
  • Check the length: hem or pin long capes and robes so nobody trips on steps.
  • Look for flame-resistant labels on bought costumes, wigs and accessories, and keep everyone away from candles in pumpkins.
  • Size up: leave room for warm layers underneath.

Where to buy costume pieces

If you'd rather buy than build, order at least a week or two before Halloween, as popular sizes sell out and shipping slows down near the date.

Abracadabra NYC, a long-standing New York costume and magic shop, sells full costumes as well as wigs, masks, hats and props, which is handy when you only need the one piece that pulls a family theme together. It's based in the US, so if you live elsewhere, check shipping times and costs to your country before you order.
