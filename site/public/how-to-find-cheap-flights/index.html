How to Find Cheap Flights From Anywhere: 7 Habits That Actually Work

Published October 17, 2026 – 5-minute read

This article contains affiliate links. If you buy through them, we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. How this works.

Cheap flights aren't about secret tricks or browsing in incognito mode. They come from a handful of habits that work wherever you live: being flexible, searching wide, watching fares before you buy and counting the real price, bags included. Here's the playbook.

Short answer: be flexible by a few days, compare several airlines and nearby airports in one search, set a price alert, book economy fares a couple of months ahead (more for peak season), and always compare the price with bags.

1. Be flexible with dates

Flying midweek, or shifting your trip by a single day, often changes the fare more than anything else. Use a calendar or month view to see the cheapest days around your ideal dates, and avoid school holidays and big festivals when you can.

2. Search wide: airlines, airports and routes

  • Compare many airlines at once. Trip.com searches airlines worldwide and is especially strong in and around Asia.
  • Check nearby airports. A second airport an hour away can be much cheaper, especially with budget airlines.
  • Try creative routes. Kiwi.com combines airlines that don't normally work together, which can unlock much cheaper itineraries. Read the transfer conditions carefully on self-transfer routes.

3. Know when to book

Summer, Christmas, EasterAs early as possible5–7 months ahead

These are rules of thumb, not guarantees: fares move with demand. Flying to Europe? See our month-by-month guide to the cheapest time to fly to Europe.

4. Set price alerts and be ready to book

Most flight search sites and apps let you set an alert for a route and dates. Watch the fare for a week or two to learn what "normal" looks like, then book when it dips. Good fares often don't last more than a day or two.

5. Count the real price

  • Bags: a cheap fare plus a paid carry-on and checked bag can cost more than a full-service fare that includes them.
  • Seats and check-in fees: some budget airlines charge for airport check-in or printed boarding passes.
  • Getting to the airport: a remote airport can add an expensive transfer or an extra hotel night.
  • Arrival time: landing at 1 a.m. may mean a taxi instead of the train.

6. Myths that waste your time

  • "Incognito mode lowers prices." There's no good evidence it does; fares change because seats sell.
  • "Tuesday is always cheapest to book." The day you fly matters far more than the day you book.
  • "Last-minute is always cheaper." For most routes, prices rise in the final weeks.

7. If your flight goes wrong

If a flight in or out of Europe arrives three or more hours late or is cancelled at short notice, you may be owed up to €600. See how to claim flight delay compensation, or check your flight for free with AirHelp.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest day to fly?

Midweek flights, often Tuesday or Wednesday, are frequently cheaper than Friday or Sunday, but it varies by route. A calendar or month view shows the cheapest days for your trip.

How far in advance should I book a flight?

As a rule of thumb, 3–8 weeks ahead for short-haul off-season trips, 2–4 months for long-haul, and 5–7 months for summer and holiday peaks.

Does incognito mode make flights cheaper?

There's no good evidence that it does. Fares change because seats sell and demand shifts, not because a site remembers your searches.

Are budget airlines always cheaper?

Not always. Once you add bags, seat selection and transfers to a remote airport, a full-service fare can work out cheaper. Compare the total price.

What is a self-transfer flight?

A trip combining separate tickets on different airlines. It can be much cheaper, but you usually collect and re-check bags yourself, and a delay on the first flight may not be covered unless the booking site offers a guarantee.
