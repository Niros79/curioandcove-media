How to Read Your Birth Chart: Sun, Moon and Rising Explained

Published October 1, 2026 – 9-minute read

Your zodiac sign is only one small piece of your astrological picture. A birth chart is a map of where the Sun, Moon and planets were at the exact moment you were born, and many people find reading theirs a surprisingly thoughtful way to reflect on who they are.

What you need

  • Your date of birth.
  • Your time of birth, as exact as possible. Check your birth certificate or ask family. The time matters because your Rising sign changes roughly every two hours.
  • Your place of birth.

Enter these into any free online birth chart calculator, and you'll get a circular chart divided into 12 slices, with planet symbols placed around it.

Start with your "Big Three"

Your Sun sign: your core self

This is the sign most people know, based on your birthday. It describes your core identity, what energizes you and how you shine.

Your Moon sign: your emotional world

The Moon moves through a new sign roughly every two and a half days, so even people born a few days apart can have different Moon signs. It describes your emotions, what makes you feel safe and how you comfort yourself.

Your Rising sign: how others first see you

Also called the Ascendant, this is the sign that was rising on the eastern horizon when you were born. It describes your outer style, first impressions and how you approach new situations. It's the main reason your birth time matters.

The planets, in one line each

  • Mercury: how you think and communicate.
  • Venus: what you love, value and find beautiful.
  • Mars: your drive, energy and how you handle conflict.
  • Jupiter: where you grow and find opportunity.
  • Saturn: responsibility, discipline and life lessons.
  • Uranus, Neptune and Pluto: slow-moving planets linked to change, dreams and transformation, often shared by a whole generation.

To read a placement, combine the planet (what), the sign (how) and the house (where). For example, "Venus in Cancer in the 4th house" can be read as love expressed through caring, centered on home and family.

The 12 houses: the areas of life

  1. Self, appearance and fresh starts
  2. Money, possessions and values
  3. Communication, learning and siblings
  4. Home, family and roots
  5. Creativity, romance and play
  6. Daily routines, work and well-being
  7. Partnerships and close relationships
  8. Shared resources, intimacy and transformation
  9. Travel, beliefs and higher learning
  10. Career, reputation and public life
  11. Friends, community and hopes
  12. Rest, solitude and the inner world

A house with several planets in it is often an area of life that feels especially important to you.

Aspects: how the planets talk to each other

The lines across the middle of your chart show angles between planets, called aspects. The main ones:

  • Conjunction (planets together): energies blend and intensify.
  • Trine (120°) and sextile (60°): easy, supportive connections.
  • Square (90°) and opposition (180°): tension that pushes you to grow.

How to use your chart well

  • Treat it as a mirror for reflection, not a rulebook. Notice what resonates and leave what doesn't.
  • Journal about one placement at a time instead of trying to read everything at once.
  • Don't base decisions about health, money or relationships on astrology alone.

Some people enjoy talking their chart through with an experienced astrologer. If you try a reading, choose a well-known platform with transparent pricing and reviews, set a budget in advance, and treat it as a conversation for reflection and fun rather than a prediction.

Astrology is a tradition of symbolism and reflection, not a science. Enjoy it as a way to think about yourself, and look after the big decisions with practical advice.

Many people like to keep notes on what they notice about their chart over time. If that sounds like you, try a few of our 30 journaling prompts as a starting point.

Quick recap

  • You need your birth date, exact time and place.
  • Start with your Sun, Moon and Rising signs.
  • Read placements as planet (what) + sign (how) + house (where).
