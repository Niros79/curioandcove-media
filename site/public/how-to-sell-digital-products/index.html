How to Sell Digital Products Online: A Beginner's Guide (2026)

Published October 20, 2026 – 11-minute read

This article contains affiliate links. If you buy through them, we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. How this works.

Short answer: pick one small problem you can solve, turn it into a simple file (a guide, template, planner or itinerary), check that people actually want it, put it on a store that handles payments and delivery for you, and send people to it from an email list and Pinterest. You can start for very little, and you don't need a big audience to make your first sale.

What counts as a digital product?

Anything people can download or access online without you shipping a box. The most common types for beginners:

  • Guides and ebooks: a short PDF that solves one specific problem.
  • Templates: budgets, content calendars, resumes, Notion or spreadsheet systems.
  • Printables and planners: trip planners, packing lists, habit trackers, kids' activity sheets.
  • Travel itineraries and city guides: a day-by-day plan with maps and booking tips.
  • Photo presets, stock photos, music or sound packs if you create visual or audio work.
  • Mini-courses: a few short videos or lessons on one skill.
  • Memberships: a monthly download or private content for subscribers, once you have an audience.

The big advantage: you make it once and can sell it many times, with no stock and no shipping.

Step 1: Choose one problem, for one kind of person

The best first product is narrow. "A travel guide" is hard to sell; "a printable 7-day packing list for families flying with a toddler" is easy to understand and easy to buy. Ask yourself:

  • What do people regularly ask me about?
  • What did I struggle with that I now know how to do?
  • What have I already made for myself (a spreadsheet, a checklist, an itinerary) that others would find useful?

Step 2: Validate it before you build it

Spend an evening checking that people want it. Three quick, free ways:

  1. Search the question. Look on Pinterest, Reddit, Quora and Google for the problem. Lots of questions and "people also ask" results are a good sign.
  2. Look for paid competitors. If similar products sell on marketplaces, that's proof of demand, not a reason to give up. Your angle can be more specific or better designed.
  3. Ask your list or followers. Even 20 people answering "would this help you?" teaches you a lot. If you don't have a list yet, start one: see how to start a newsletter side hustle.

Step 3: Create a simple first version

Keep version one short and genuinely useful. A 10-page guide that solves the problem beats a 100-page book nobody finishes.

  • Use free tools you already have: Google Docs, Canva or a spreadsheet are enough.
  • A free AI assistant can help you outline, edit and brainstorm examples, but check every fact and add your own experience. Our list of everyday tasks a free AI assistant can do has prompts you can adapt.
  • For video or audio products, AI voice tools such as ElevenLabs can produce a clean voiceover if you don't want to record your own voice.
  • Export to PDF (or the right file type), test it on a phone, and fix anything that's hard to read.

Step 4: Price it

Most beginner digital products sell somewhere between a few dollars and a few dozen. A simple way to decide:

ProductTypical starting range
Single printable or checklistLow (a few dollars)
Template pack or detailed guideMid (roughly $10–$30)
Mini-course or complete systemHigher (from about $30 up)

Price for the result, not the page count. If your itinerary saves someone hours of research, that's what they're paying for. You can always raise the price once you have reviews.

Step 5: Choose where to sell

You have three main options:

  • Marketplaces (for example Etsy for printables): built-in traffic, but more competition and fees per sale, and the customers belong to the marketplace.
  • Simple creator platforms with a free tier: quick to start, usually take a percentage of each sale.
  • Your own store: a hosted storefront with your branding, where you keep your customers' emails and pay a flat monthly fee.

Step 6: Set up your product page

  • A clear title that says who it's for and what it does.
  • 3–5 bullet points with the outcomes, not just the contents.
  • Preview images: show real pages, on a phone and on paper.
  • What's included (file type, number of pages, how they get it).
  • A short FAQ and your refund policy.

Step 7: Get your first sales without paid ads

  1. Email list. Your best customers are people who already trust you. Offer a free checklist to build the list, then tell subscribers about the product. A free tier on a newsletter platform such as Kit is enough to start.
  2. Pinterest. Pins keep sending visitors for months. Make several pins per product with different headlines, linking to a helpful blog post or straight to the product page.
  3. Helpful answers. Answer real questions on Quora, Reddit or Facebook groups. Help first, and mention your product only where it genuinely fits and the community rules allow it.
  4. A launch discount. A short-term code for your list creates a reason to buy now.
  5. Your own blog or site. Write articles that solve related problems and recommend your product at the end. See how to build a website for a side project with AI, and if you write about travel, how a travel blog makes money.

Taxes, business setup and other boring (important) bits

  • Sales tax and VAT: rules for digital products depend on where your buyers live. Check what your platform collects for you and what you're responsible for.
  • Income tax: earnings from digital products are usually taxable income where you live. Keep simple records from day one.
  • Business entity: many sellers start as individuals. If you later want a US company (for example, if you live outside the US and sell mainly to Americans), services like doola can help you form an LLC. Get local tax advice before you decide.
  • Copyright: only sell what you created or have the rights to sell, including images and fonts.

A realistic first-month plan

  • Week 1: pick the problem, validate it, write the outline.
  • Week 2: create version one and the preview images.
  • Week 3: set up the store and product page, and test a purchase yourself.
  • Week 4: email your list, publish 5–10 pins and one helpful blog post, then collect feedback and improve.

Your first sale matters more than your first big launch. Once one product sells, make a second for the same people.

Frequently asked questions

What digital products sell best for beginners?

Narrow, practical products that save time: templates, checklists, printables, short guides and itineraries. One clear problem for one kind of person sells better than a broad topic.

Do I need an audience to sell digital products?

It helps, but you can start small. An email list of a few dozen people, a few Pinterest pins and helpful answers on forums can bring your first sales.

How much does Sellfy cost?

At the time of writing, Sellfy plans start at $29 a month, or less with yearly billing, with a 14-day free trial and 0% Sellfy transaction fees. Payment processor fees still apply. Check Sellfy's pricing page for current plans.

Is Etsy or my own store better for digital products?

A marketplace like Etsy brings built-in traffic but more competition and per-sale fees. Your own store, for example on Sellfy, gives you branding and your customers' emails for a monthly fee. Many sellers use both.

Do I have to pay tax on digital product sales?

Usually yes. Income is generally taxable where you live, and sales tax or VAT rules depend on where buyers are. Check what your platform handles and get local advice.
