20 Screen-Free Family Evening Ideas for Fall

Published September 30, 2026 · Updated October 16, 2026 – 6-minute read

This article contains affiliate links. If you buy through them, we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. How this works.

As the evenings get darker, it's easy for everyone to disappear into their own screen. One or two screen-free evenings a week can become the ones your family remembers most. Here are twenty ideas that cost little or nothing.

Cozy nights in

  1. Family book night. Everyone reads their own book in the same room, with blankets and hot cocoa. Younger kids can be read to.
  2. Board game tournament. Keep a simple scoreboard on the fridge for the whole season.
  3. Build a blanket fort and eat dinner inside it.
  4. Jigsaw puzzle week. Leave a puzzle out on a table and let everyone add pieces whenever they pass by.
  5. Story circle. One person starts a story with a single sentence, and everyone adds the next line.
  6. Music night. Take turns choosing songs and explaining why you love them. Parents' old favourites are often a hit.

In the kitchen

  1. Homemade pizza night. Everyone builds their own with a few simple toppings.
  2. Bake an apple crisp or apple muffins together. Kids can measure and stir.
  3. Apple tasting. Buy three or four varieties and rank them from sweetest to most sour.
  4. Start a family recipe book. Write down one favourite recipe each week, with a note about who loves it.

Crafts and creative evenings

  1. Leaf rubbings. Place leaves under paper and rub over them with the side of a crayon.
  2. Paint pumpkins, then hold a costume try-on. Painting is safer and easier for small children. Afterwards, pull out old clothes and accessories and test Halloween ideas. Need inspiration? Here are 15 easy family costume ideas, and Abracadabra has ready-made costumes and accessories if you're missing a piece.
  3. Make a leaf garland with leaves collected on a walk, pressed for a few days in a heavy book.
  4. Put on a puppet show with socks, paper bags or a cardboard box theatre.
  5. Write letters to grandparents or friends far away, with drawings included.

Outside, while there's still light

  1. Nature scavenger hunt. Find a red leaf, a pinecone, a smooth stone, something soft and something that smells good.
  2. Backyard campfire or fire pit with s'mores, where it's safe and allowed.
  3. Stargazing. Autumn nights are dark early. Bundle up, lie on a blanket and look for constellations.

Slow and reflective

  1. Gratitude jar. Each person adds a note about something good from their week, and you read them together at Thanksgiving or New Year.
  2. Plan next weekend together. Let each family member suggest one thing, and fit in as many as you can.

Bonus idea: grow something together. Plant a few herbs on a sunny windowsill and let the kids water and harvest them. Our windowsill herb guide covers the easiest ones, or a smart indoor garden like Click & Grow handles the watering and light for you.

How to make it stick

  • Pick a fixed night, like "Screen-Free Thursdays", so it becomes a routine rather than a negotiation.
  • Parents go screen-free too. Kids notice immediately if the rule only applies to them.
  • Put phones in a basket by the door for the evening.
  • Let kids choose the activity sometimes. They'll look forward to it more.
  • Keep it short at first. Even one screen-free hour counts.

Quick recap

  • Choose a regular night and include the whole family, adults too.
  • Mix cozy indoor ideas with a little outdoor time while the light lasts.
  • Keep it simple, cheap and fun rather than perfect.
