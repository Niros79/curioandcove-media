The Complete Trip Planning Checklist: What to Book and When

Published October 3, 2026 – 11-minute read

This article contains affiliate links. If you buy through them, we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. How this works.

Most trip planning stress comes from doing things in the wrong order: booking a hotel before the flight, buying insurance after something has already gone wrong, or landing at midnight with no data and no ride. This checklist puts every step in the order that actually works, wherever you live and wherever you're going.

Use it as a timeline. Start at the top a couple of months before you leave, and tick things off as you go. At the end there's a short version you can save for your next trip.

2 to 3 months before: the big bookings

1. Lock in your dates and book the flight first

Flights are usually the most expensive and least flexible part of a trip, so they come first. Everything else, from hotel nights to car rental dates, depends on when you actually land.

  • Be flexible by a day or two. Flying midweek or shifting your trip by a single day can change the fare noticeably. Most search sites show a calendar view with prices for nearby dates.
  • Compare more than one airline. A search that covers many airlines at once saves time. Trip.com compares flights worldwide and is especially strong for routes in and around Asia. Kiwi.com is useful for creative combinations of airlines that other sites don't put together.
  • Read the baggage rules before you pay. A cheap fare with a paid carry-on can end up costing more than a slightly higher fare that includes it.
  • Check the arrival time. A flight landing at 1 a.m. may need a pre-booked transfer and an extra hotel night, which changes the real price.

2. Book your stay, with free cancellation

Once the flight is fixed, book where you'll sleep. In busy seasons and popular cities, the best-value places in good locations go first.

  • Choose location over size. A smaller room near public transport or the old town saves time and taxi money every single day.
  • Pick free cancellation when you can. It costs little or nothing extra and lets you rebook if prices drop or plans change.
  • Read recent reviews, especially comments about noise, cleanliness and check-in times.

You can compare hotels, apartments and resorts worldwide on Trip.com, often with flight and hotel in the same account, which makes changes easier to manage.

3. Check passports, visas and travel permits

Do this early, because some documents take weeks.

  • Passport validity: many countries require your passport to be valid for at least three to six months after your return date, with a couple of blank pages.
  • Visas and electronic travel permits: more and more countries ask visa-free visitors to apply online before departure. The United States (ESTA) and the United Kingdom (ETA) are two examples, and the rules change regularly.
  • Check the official government site of the country you're visiting, not a third-party agency that charges extra for the same form.

About 1 month before: protect the trip

4. Buy travel insurance soon after booking

Travel insurance is the step most people leave too late. Buying it soon after you book matters, because cancellation cover usually only protects you against things that happen after you bought the policy.

A good policy typically covers:

  • Medical emergencies abroad, which can be extremely expensive in some countries, along with emergency transport home.
  • Trip cancellation or interruption for covered reasons such as illness.
  • Lost or delayed luggage and essential purchases while you wait.

Before you buy, check the coverage limits, the excess (the part you pay yourself), and whether activities you plan, such as skiing or diving, are included. Get quotes from two or three insurers and compare them on these points, not only on price.

5. Book the tours and tickets that sell out

You don't need to plan every hour, but a handful of things book up weeks ahead in popular destinations: famous museums and monuments with timed entry, day trips from big cities, cooking classes and small-group tours.

  • Book the one or two things you'd hate to miss, and leave the rest of the days loose.
  • Look for free cancellation up to 24 hours before, so the weather can't ruin your plans.
  • Skip-the-line tickets are often worth it at the busiest sights in high season.

The Trip.com attractions and tours section covers tickets, day trips and experiences in cities around the world. Viator is worth comparing too: most of its tours have free cancellation up to 24 hours before.

6. Reserve a rental car or plan your trains

If you'll explore beyond the city, decide how you'll get around now. Rental prices tend to climb close to the date, especially for automatic cars and in summer.

  • Check the deposit and insurance excess. These vary a lot between companies and can tie up a large amount on your card.
  • Read the fuel and mileage policy and whether you can cross borders with the car.
  • Consider local companies. Localrent lists cars from local rental firms in many holiday destinations, often with no deposit or a low one. Trip.com compares car rentals from larger brands.
  • Prefer trains? In much of Europe and Asia, trains are faster than driving between cities. You can search and book train tickets on Trip.com for many countries.

1 to 2 weeks before: the details that save you on arrival

7. Sort out mobile data

Landing without data means no maps, no ride apps and no way to message your host. Roaming with your home plan can be very expensive, so it's worth deciding before you go.

  • Check your current plan. Some plans include roaming in certain countries; many charge high daily fees.
  • Local SIM on arrival: often cheap, but you'll need to find a shop and may need your passport.
  • Travel SIM before you leave: you arrive already connected. Drimsim is a single SIM card that works in 150+ countries, handy if you travel often or visit several countries in one trip. It's a physical SIM, so order it early enough for delivery and make sure your phone is unlocked.

8. Book your airport transfer

The first hour after landing is when tired travelers overpay the most. A pre-booked transfer means a fixed price and a driver waiting at arrivals, which is especially worth it for late-night flights, families with kids and luggage, or cities where airport taxis are known for surprises.

GetTransfer lets you book a private driver in advance at an agreed price in many countries. If you're arriving at a reasonable hour with light bags, an airport train or bus is often the cheapest option, so check that first.

9. Get your money ready

  • Use a card without foreign transaction fees, or a multi-currency card, to avoid paying a few percent extra on every purchase.
  • Tell your bank you're traveling if it still asks, so your card isn't blocked on day one.
  • Always pay in the local currency when a card machine offers to convert for you. The conversion offered at the terminal is usually worse.
  • Carry a small amount of local cash for taxis, markets and tips.

10. Save copies of everything

Take photos of your passport, insurance policy and bookings, and store them somewhere you can reach offline. Download offline maps for the cities you'll visit. If your phone gets lost, these copies make everything easier.

Travel day

  • Check in online and save your boarding pass to your phone.
  • Keep medication, chargers, documents and one change of clothes in your carry-on.
  • Get to the airport early for international flights, especially in peak season.
  • Keep your boarding pass until the trip is over. You may need it if something goes wrong.

If your flight goes wrong

Delays and cancellations happen, but they can come with money attached. Under European rules, if your flight from the EU, or into the EU on a European airline, arrives three or more hours late, is cancelled at short notice or is overbooked, you may be owed up to €600 per passenger, regardless of your nationality. Your trip planning checklist

Save this short version for next time:

  • 2 to 3 months before: book flights, book a stay with free cancellation, check passport validity and entry rules.
  • About 1 month before: buy travel insurance, book must-see tours and tickets, reserve a car or plan trains.
  • 1 to 2 weeks before: arrange mobile data, book the airport transfer, prepare cards and some cash, save copies of documents and offline maps.
  • Travel day: online check-in, essentials in your carry-on, keep your boarding pass.
  • If it goes wrong: check whether you're owed compensation.
