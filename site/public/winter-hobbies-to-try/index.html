15 Cosy Winter Hobbies to Try at Home (And a Few That Can Earn Money)

Published October 23, 2026 – 7 min read

This article contains affiliate links. If you buy through them, we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. How this works.

Long winter evenings are the perfect time to pick up something new with your hands. The best winter hobbies are cosy, cheap to start and give you something to show for your time. Here are fifteen ideas to try at home, and how a few of them can even pay for themselves.

15 cosy winter hobbies

1. Crochet or knitting

Cheap to start, portable, and you end up with something warm. See our crochet for beginners guide.

2. Watercolour painting

A small paint set, a pad of proper watercolour paper and one brush are enough. Winter scenes are perfect practice.

3. Journaling

Ten minutes in the evening with a notebook. Our journaling prompts help you start.

4. Baking bread

Slow, warm and rewarding. A basic loaf needs flour, water, yeast, salt and patience.

5. Indoor gardening

Microgreens and herbs grow on a windowsill all winter. See our indoor winter garden guide.

6. Photography

Short days mean golden light comes early. Practise with your phone, then learn composition and editing.

7. Jigsaw puzzles

A big puzzle on the table is a slow, shared family project.

8. Candle making

Soy wax, wicks and a pouring jug are all you need. Great for homemade gifts.

9. Embroidery

A hoop, a needle and some thread. Small projects finish in an evening.

10. Learning a language

Fifteen minutes a day before your next trip adds up quickly.

11. Reading challenges

Set a theme for the winter: classics, a country per month, or books under 200 pages.

12. Calligraphy and hand lettering

Pretty for cards and labels, and very calming.

13. Cooking a cuisine from scratch

Pick one country and cook a new dish from it every weekend.

14. Paper crafts and card making

Make your own Christmas and birthday cards for the year ahead.

15. Digital art

A tablet and a free app can turn doodles into prints, stickers or wallpapers.

How to choose a hobby you'll stick with

  • Start small and cheap. Buy the basics, not the full kit, until you know you enjoy it.
  • Pick one that fits your evenings. Portable hobbies like crochet suit busy households; messy ones like painting need a corner you can leave set up.
  • Learn from a short course. A few good lessons save months of trial and error. Learning with Experts runs online courses in photography, gardening and more, with feedback from the tutors.
  • Make it social. A weekly craft night with friends or a local class keeps you going.

Turning a hobby into a little side income

Not every hobby needs to make money, and keeping some just for fun is healthy. But if you enjoy making things, a few winter hobbies sell well:

  • Digital patterns and templates: crochet patterns, embroidery designs, printable planners and colouring pages can be sold again and again without making anything new. A simple storefront like Sellfy handles payments and delivers files automatically.
  • Handmade gifts before Christmas: candles, cards and small knitted items sell well at local markets in December.
  • Prints and photos: digital art and photography can become prints, wallpapers or stock photos.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest hobby to start in winter?

Journaling, reading challenges, jigsaw puzzles and crochet all cost very little to start. You need a notebook, a library card, a puzzle or a hook and some yarn.

Which hobbies can make money?

Crafts that can become digital products, such as crochet patterns, printables and digital art, are the easiest to sell repeatedly. Handmade items like candles and cards sell well before Christmas.

How do I stick with a new hobby?

Start small, schedule a regular time for it, learn the basics from a short course, and share it with friends.
