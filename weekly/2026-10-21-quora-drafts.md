## 1. Can beginners really make money selling digital products online? (CU link)
https://www.quora.com/Can-beginners-really-make-money-selling-digital-products-online

Yes, but usually not the way the hype videos suggest. Beginners who make their first sales tend to do three things differently: they pick a narrow problem, they test demand before building, and they sell to people who already trust them.

A narrow product is easier to sell than a broad one. "A travel guide" is hard; "a printable packing list for families flying with a toddler" is easy to understand and easy to buy. Think about what people already ask you, or what you made for yourself (a spreadsheet, a checklist, an itinerary) that others would find useful.

Before you build anything, spend an evening checking demand: search the problem on Pinterest, Reddit, Quora and Google, and look for similar paid products. Competition is proof that people pay, not a reason to quit.

Keep version one short. A 10-page guide that solves the problem beats a 100-page ebook nobody finishes. Free tools like Google Docs or Canva are enough.

Then send people to it from places that keep working: a small email list (offer a free checklist to start it), a few Pinterest pins per product, and genuinely helpful answers in communities like this one.

Expect your first sales to be small. The real win is learning what your audience buys, then making a second product for the same people.

## 2. What are the main benefits of using an eSIM while traveling? (FĂRĂ link)
https://www.quora.com/What-are-the-main-benefits-of-using-an-eSIM-while-traveling-I-ve-been-hearing-a-lot-about-eSIMs-lately-Why-are-they-useful-compared-to-a-phys

The biggest benefit is that you have mobile data the moment you land, without hunting for a SIM shop or paying your home carrier's roaming fees.

A few other practical advantages:

You set it up at home. You buy the plan in an app, install it on Wi-Fi before you fly, and switch it on when you arrive.
Your home SIM stays in the phone. You keep your number for calls and texts, and WhatsApp keeps working because it's tied to your number, not your SIM.
No tiny plastic card to lose or swap, and no tools to open the SIM tray.
Easy multi-country trips. Regional plans cover several countries with one eSIM.
You can top up from the app if you run low.

The trade-offs: your phone must support eSIM and be carrier-unlocked, and most travel eSIMs are data-only, so you call through apps rather than getting a local number.

Two tips: turn off data roaming on your home SIM when you switch to the eSIM, and don't delete the eSIM until you're home in case you need a top-up. As a rough guide, maps, chat and email use about 3–5 GB a week; add social media and photos and it's more like 5–10 GB.
